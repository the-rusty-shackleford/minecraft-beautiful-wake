# D-0007 — The wake is drawn through the particle shader; the trail samples start-of-tick positions on a clock of its own; smoothness is measured

**Decision**: Every wake texture is drawn on a render type of our own
over the game's particle shader (position, texture, colour, light;
translucent; no depth write; back faces culled), not the entity
translucent type. The tracker samples the hull's start-of-tick position
(`entity.xo`, `zo`), the same point the renderer's interpolation starts
from, on a client-tick clock of the mod's own. A swimmer stays a swimmer
until its eye is half a block under. A swimmer's foam is boosted 1.8
times and has a wash of white water round the body. Every sample is a
row (no thinning); the head sample's texture time is the frame's; fades
and ring ages are fractional; the trough clamp is soft; every ramp is a
smoothstep; the head's heading reaches two samples back; vertices go to
the camera in doubles. `WakeMeshTest.aSteadyRunIsSmoothFrameToFrame`
builds the mesh over seventeen frames of a steady run and asserts that
no row over a sample vanishes and returns, that positions and alphas
move evenly, and that nothing reverses direction more than twice.

**Why**: Rusty: "Everything is jittery, as if its all being redrawn a
bajillion times instead of additively being drawn", "the wake behind
players went dark again", then, of the run under Complementary with all
of this in place, "Thats it. Thats the win". Measured causes of the
jitter: thinning chose rows by list position, so the row set flipped
each time the oldest sample was pruned; the tracker sampled the
end-of-tick position while the head was interpolated from the start, so
every row's distance behind the hull sawtoothed by two ticks' travel;
the hull's part-tick head entered the speed windows as a slow step; the
churn's texture time on the head snapped once a tick; ages were whole
ticks; the trough clamp and every ramp had corners. The swimmer's wake
under Complementary was found by experiment, not reasoning: forcing
vertex alpha opaque brought it back, forcing full-bright light did not,
and holding alpha at a half did -- the pack's entity program pushes any
vertex with alpha under a half behind what is beneath it, and a
swimmer's faded wake lay entirely under that line. The particle program
has no such branch, no normal to flip, and draws translucency at every
alpha; its one colour heuristic that could bite (pure white at middling
alpha read as snow) is avoided with a white of 254 and a shade floor of
0.6.

**Alternatives rejected**: *Keeping alpha above a half on the entity
path* -- the far V and the churn's tail would have ended in a cut.
*Compensating the pack's depth push with a lift* -- pack-specific and
floating in vanilla. *Thinning by sample identity* -- fixes the flip but
not the pops; the table made thinning unnecessary.
