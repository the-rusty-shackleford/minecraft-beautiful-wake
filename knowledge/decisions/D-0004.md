# D-0004 — The wake is a surface in relief: a height field meshed over the water, shaded, edged and lined by texture, with a bubble burst at the bow

**Decision**: The wake is a `WakeField`: a height and a foam coverage at
every point in the hull's frame (`d` behind the hull's centre, `s`
across). The height is four terms -- a bow wave `BOW_HEIGHT` 0.2 tall at
the bow, a stern trough 0.1 deep, transverse ripples behind the stern
that die within a few wavelengths, and the chevrons: ridges 0.12 tall
running back and outward at 42 degrees from the track, 3.0 blocks apart
across them, points toward the hull, dying over 22 blocks -- all inside a
V that opens at the Kelvin angle from a little over half a hull wide at
the centre and narrows to a point 0.9 hulls ahead. `WakeMesh` lays a
grid over the trail, one row per sample (thinned with age: every one to
24 ticks, every second to 60, every third after) plus five ahead of the
hull, 25 columns across each from edge to edge, every vertex at the
water's height there with its normal, and on the inside of a turn a row
stops short of the turn's radius so rows never fold. Heights and slopes
come from `WakeTable`, the field sampled at 0.1 blocks once per hull
width, built off the render thread. The renderer draws the mesh three
times with the entity translucent type: the *skin* (a pale sheet, RGBA
188/224/252/108, fuller down the middle, with a white line a tenth of a
block wide on the V's edge, mapped by the distance from the edge so the
line is crisp whatever the grid), the *lines* (white along every chevron
ridge, mapped by the chevron phase, faded out until the chevrons start),
and the *foam* (a cloud of round bubbles, pinned to the water, as opaque
as the churn and the bow's shoulders are). Each skin vertex is shaded by
its normal against a fixed high light, 0.55 to 1.25, so ridges read as
ridges whatever a renderer makes of the normals. The bow throws
`BowFoam`: up to twelve bubbles a tick, most small and a few large, off
the hull's shoulders and a few from the churn, up and outward, falling
under gravity, settling on the water and popping; drawn as billboards.
The pattern stands still in the hull's frame, as a wake's does.

**Why**: Rusty, on 1.1.0 in the pack under Iris: "the wake is 2D, its
fuckin flat! It should actually displace water and what not. The white of
the wake doesn't even look like foam, its just messy", then "Compare to
windwaker" with a screenshot of the King of Red Lions, then "a weird
pattern directly under the boat, like a square or rectangle that follows
the boat". Three things followed. *Relief*: the water has to stand up, so
the wake is a surface with height and normals, not quads on a plane; the
vanilla water mesh cannot be displaced from a mod, so the wake is drawn
as its own surface over the water, its troughs sinking out of sight and
its crests standing proud, its shading baked so the relief shows even
where a shader lights entity geometry flat. *Wind Waker*: its trail is a
pale blue band, not white, with crisp white lines along its edges and
nested chevrons pointing at the boat, and its bow foam is a burst of
round bubbles in three dimensions -- so the sheet is pale and edged, the
chevron ridges carry white lines and point forward (a real Kelvin wake's
diverging crests do the same), and the bow throws bubbles as billboards.
*The rectangle*: the old bow crest was a hull-wide quad ahead of the bow;
the V now comes to a point at the bow so nothing squared-off shows. The
old flat white smear is gone with the old geometry. Performance: the
analytic field is a dozen exponentials a point and a mesh was 1 ms per
craft per frame; the table and the thinning bring it to 0.08 ms.

**Alternatives rejected**: *Displacing the water block mesh* -- not
reachable from a mod without a mixin into chunk rendering and every
shader pack's water. *Normals alone for the relief* -- vanilla lights
entity geometry by them, but a shader pack may not, and the shading has
to survive both. *A single texture with the lines and the sheet* -- a
quad spanning the start of the chevrons would sweep several lines into
one; the lines are their own pass with their own fade. *Particles for
the bow foam* -- the count needed for a Wind Waker burst is hundreds a
second and they cannot be shaped; billboards from a small simulation are
cheap and look right.

**Follow-ups**: the look under Iris in the pack, which the booth cannot
show; the outer sheet on the outside of a hard turn, which is correct
but broad; a swell to the sheet along a long straight run.
