# D-0005 — Foam is pixels at the game's density, uneven and animated; the wake is scaled to a rowboat; a splash foams by its weight

**Decision**: Everything white is drawn at sixteen pixels to the block
with hard edges, as the game draws everything else: the V's edge line
two pixels wide, stepping in and out by a pixel along its length; the
chevron lines the same; the churn a cloud of one-to-four-pixel bubbles
with a pale rim inside each; the bow's bubbles sixteen-pixel discs; a
splash's foam one- and two-pixel flecks. Each of the edge, the lines and
the churn has two frames with the pixels stepped differently, shown
turn and turn about every four ticks; the flecks have three, cycled
every three ticks from a frame and a quarter-turn chosen by the
splash's birth tick, a splash over 1.6 getting a second square a
quarter further round. A splash's foam alpha is nothing under a
strength of 0.8, everything at 2.2, and outlasts the ring a little. The
boat's outline nose ends 0.45 hulls ahead of the centre, under the bow
where the model hides it; a swimmer's 0.35, inside the body; the bow's
foam is the shoulders' patch, at full coverage and streaming back along
the hull's sides. `wake.scale` (default 0.65) fades the sheet and its
lines with distance behind the hull (to a third at 32 blocks times the
scale), scales the relief and the bow's bubble count, and leaves the
V's angle and its width at the hull alone.

**Why**: Rusty, on 2.0.0: "the wake looks good but its a bit too large
... a small rowboat moving at that speed would generate some wake for
sure but not quite that much"; "when items are dropped into water, the
ripple created should have a bit of white pixels that help accent the
circular ripple pattern to simulate a bit of foam from the splash.
Maybe a gentle drop does not really produce the foam whereas a heavier
drop would produce a good little bit"; a screenshot of a wader with
chunky, uneven foam round the legs -- "see how the pixel foam is not
perfectly symmetrical? Makes it feel dynamic"; "there's like a triangle
that stays under the player ... before there was a rectangle, now its a
triangle"; and a screenshot of the boat's bow -- "see the foam action
going on there? That looks great, I like that in place of the bow crest".
The triangle was the V's point ahead of the body, hidden under a boat
but not a wader; the nose now hides under both. The reference foam was
the game's own pixel density, uneven, and changing as the player moved:
smooth textures at four times that density looked airbrushed next to
it, so the foam is drawn at the game's density and animated by stepped
frames, which costs nothing per frame beyond picking a texture.

**Alternatives rejected**: *Shrinking the Kelvin angle* to make the
wake smaller -- the angle is the one thing about a wake that is always
true; the sheet fades sooner instead. *Randomising the flecks per frame
on the CPU* -- a texture per frame is free and deterministic. *Particles
for the splash foam* -- they cannot ride the ring's crest.

**Follow-ups**: the look under Iris in the pack, still only Rusty can
judge; a ring that is not a perfect circle.
